The difference between USB data and power cables

When shopping for cables, it is easy to assume any cord with the right connector will do everything you need, but packaging can be deceiving. A standard power-only cable is built strictly to deliver electricity from the power block to your device. In contrast, a true data and power cable contains internal wiring capable of acting as a two-way highway, allowing files, photos, and system commands to travel back and forth at high speeds while simultaneously providing power. When reading packaging, look for terms like "Sync & Charge," explicit data transfer speeds (such as 10, 20 or 40 Gbps), or specific protocol logos like an USB-IF certification to ensure your cable isn't secretly choking your file transfers down to a crawl. A fast data cable is great for moving videos and photos off your phone or tablet and on to your computer.

USB's birth in 1996

Universal Serial Bus technology began at a modest speed of 1.5 Mbps, followed quickly by USB 1.1 at 12 Mbps. When USB 2.0 arrived in April 2000 with a significantly faster 480 Mbps data rate (40 times faster than USB 1.1), it established the foundation and starting point from which every subsequent generation began consistently doubling in speed.

The Jump from USB 2.0 to USB 3.0

For years, USB 2.0 was the standard plug for connecting accessories and external storage. It worked fine for basic mice and keyboards but trying to move a large photo album or movie files took ages, and a massive bottleneck formed as file sizes grew over time.

That bottleneck shattered with the release of USB 3.0. Bumping the speed up to 5 Gbps, USB 3.0 is 10.4 times faster than USB 2.0. Beyond raw speed, it introduced two-way communication, allowing devices to read and write data at the exact same time and it could charge a phone or tablet twice as fast.

The Era of Constant Doubling: USB 3.1 to USB4

Following the massive leap of USB 3.0, the industry settled into a predictable pattern where each new flagship version simply doubled the speed limit of the one right before it.

First came USB 3.1 (Gen 2) with a top speed of 10 Gbps, making it 2 times faster than USB 3.0 (and over 20 times faster than old USB 2.0). Next, USB 3.2 (Gen 2x2) debuted in 2019 by utilizing dual-lane pathways over the modern oval-shaped USB-C cable. Pushing speeds to 20 Gbps, USB 3.2 is 2 times faster than USB 3.1.

When the architecture upgraded to the modern USB4 standard, the pattern held firm. It still used the same USB-C type connector but now had a peak capability of 40 Gbps. USB4 is 2 times faster than USB 3.2, becoming the mainstream standard for modern laptops and desktops.

The Modern Era - USB4 Version 2.0 and Beyond

To avoid confusing decimal points, the latest evolution is named USB4 Version 2.0.

Sticking to the golden rule of performance, USB4 Version 2.0 tops out at a blazing fast 80 Gbps, meaning USB4 Version 2.0 is 2 times faster than original USB4. In specialized setups, it can even flex up to 120 Gbps for high-end display outputs and can be used for fast charging phones and tablets.

When shopping for a new computer or external drive today, look for USB4 or higher to ensure your file transfers stay fast and future-proof.
