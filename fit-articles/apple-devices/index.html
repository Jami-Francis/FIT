Apple SMC & NVRAM, Wi-Fi dropping

Reset SMC or NVRAM

Check if MacBook / Air has an Intel processor or Apple Silicon (M1, M2, or M3 chips) at Apple Menu > About This Mac.

For Apple Silicon (M1, M2, M3): There is no traditional SMC or manual NVRAM reset key combo. The hardware performs a self-check and refreshes these settings every time the computer is fully shut down.

  • Shut Down your Mac completely.
  • Close the lid for about 30 seconds.
  • Open the lid and turn it back on. This is the official equivalent of a reset for these models.

For Intel-based Macs:

  1. NVRAM / PRAM Reset (for settings like speaker volume, screen resolution, and startup-disk selection):
    • Shut down your Mac.
    • Turn it on and immediately press and hold these four keys together: Option + Command + P + R for 20 seconds.
    • Release after the second chime, OR if it has the T2 Security Chip, release after the Apple logo appears and disappears for the second time.
  2. SMC Reset (for power management, fans, and Wi-Fi hardware communication):
    • For models from 2018 to 2020 (with T2 Chip): Shut down your Mac. On the built-in keyboard, press and hold Control (left) + Option (left) + Shift (right) for 7 seconds. Then also press and hold the Power button. Hold all four keys for another 7 seconds, release, wait a few seconds, and press the Power button to turn it back on.
    • For older models (2017 and earlier): Shut down the Mac and ensure it's plugged into power. On the left side of the keyboard, press Shift + Control + Option and the Power button at the same time. Release all keys at once, then press the Power button to restart.

Common "Gotcha": If you use a firmware password, you must turn it off before these key combinations will work.

Wi-Fi Dropping After Connecting

Network configuration conflicts, DHCP lease expiration, or aggressive power-saving features are common culprits. Try these steps in order:

  1. Renew the DHCP Lease
    • Go to System Settings (or System Preferences) > Network.
    • Select Wi-Fi and click Details (or Advanced) next to the network.
    • Click TCP/IP in the sidebar.
    • Click Renew DHCP Lease, then click OK and Apply.
  2. Create a "New Network Location" (rebuilds network preferences settings for Location)
    • In Network settings, look for the Locations dropdown (may need to click a "..." or "More" button).
    • Choose Edit Locations... and click the [+] icon.
    • Rename it (e.g., "Home" or "Fix") and click Done.
    • Select this new location in the dropdown and click Apply.
  3. Clear System Configuration Files (deleting configuration plists)
    • Turn Wi-Fi OFF.
    • Open Finder and press Cmd + Shift + G.
    • Paste the path: /Library/Preferences/SystemConfiguration/
    • Find and delete (or move to a backup folder on your desktop) these files:
      • com.apple.airport.preferences.plist
      • com.apple.network.eapolclient.configuration.plist
      • com.apple.wifi.message-tracer.plist
      • NetworkInterfaces.plist
      • preferences.plist
    • Restart your Mac and turn Wi-Fi back on.
  4. Adjust Router Band Settings
    • If your router uses the same name (SSID) for both 2.4GHz and 5GHz, your Mac might continuously try to hop between them when the signal fluctuates.
    • The Fix: Give them different names (e.g., "Home_WiFi" and "Home_WiFi_5G"). Connect specifically to the 5GHz band for better stability, or the 2.4GHz if you are far from the router.
  5. Check for Hardware Interference
    • USB 3.0 devices and poorly shielded hubs can emit radio frequency interference that kills Wi-Fi signals on MacBooks.
    • If you have dongles, external hard drives, or monitors plugged in, try unplugging them for 30 minutes to test stability.
  6. Use Wireless Diagnostics (open hidden tool to watch the drop happen in real-time)
    • Hold the Option key and click the Wi-Fi icon in the menu bar.
    • Select Open Wireless Diagnostics.
    • Go to the top menu bar and select Window > Performance.
    • This shows a live graph of signal strength ($RSSI$) and noise. A sudden spike in "Noise" right when it drops indicates external interference.
