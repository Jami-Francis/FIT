Security risks for cellphones and tablets

Keeping your cellphone or tablet updated is essential for protecting your sensitive personal information, especially when accessing mobile banking, medical portals, password managers, and personal email or tax accounts. Without timely system software and security updates, your device is vulnerable to growing security threats.

Ideally, your device should be running the most recent operating system release—iOS 26 for Apple or Android 17 for Android—or at least receiving active periodic security patches. Older, unsupported devices face escalating security risks.

How to check your software version and updates:

  • Apple (iPhone/iPad): Open Settings, tap General, and then tap About to view your "Software Version." To check for background security updates, go back to General and tap Software Update.
  • Android: Open Settingsand scroll down. Tap on About Phone (or About Device), then select Software Information or Android Version. To check for carrier or manufacturer patches, go back to the main Settings menu and look for System Update or Security Update.

How often do updates arrive?
Manufacturer and carrier security patches typically roll out roughly every 2 to 4 months. Premium flagship devices usually receive these updates for many years, while budget models may stop receiving them much sooner.

When is it time for a new device?
Operating systems and devices that are more than 4 to 6 years old generally stop receiving critical security updates from manufacturers. If your phone falls into this age bracket, it is highly susceptible to modern cyber threats, and you should strongly consider purchasing a replacement device.

1. Unpatched Vulnerabilities
  • Zero-Day Exploits: These are "holes" in the software known to hackers but not to the phone manufacturer. Because your phone is older, the manufacturer is no longer sending "patches" to plug these holes, leaving your private data exposed.
  • Kernel Vulnerabilities: These are deep-system flaws that allow a hacker to take over the entire device, not just a single app.
2. Modern Malware (2026)
  • Banking Trojans: Threats like "GoldPickaxe" and "GodFather" are designed to steal your money. They work by creating an invisible "overlay" on top of your real banking app to steal your login details. Some can even steal your facial recognition data to bypass security.
  • MFA Bypass: Advanced malware like "MaliBot" can now intercept the one-time text codes (Multi-Factor Authentication) that your bank sends you, allowing hackers to log into your accounts even if they have your password.
3. Advanced Phishing
  • AI-Driven Scams: Hackers now use Artificial Intelligence to write incredibly convincing text messages (Smishing) and emails that look exactly like they came from your bank, UPS, or the government.
  • Quishing (QR Code Scams): Malicious QR codes are used to send you to fake websites. Modern phones have scanners that check if a link is safe, but older phones lack this protection.
4. App & Network Safety
  • Play Integrity Failures: Google and Apple have updated their security "handshakes." If your phone is too old, your banking and finance apps may detect that the device is no longer secure and will stop working entirely to protect your account.
  • Expired Certificates: Older phones have "security certificates" that eventually expire. Once they expire, your phone can no longer tell if a Public Wi-Fi connection (at an airport or hotel) is genuine or a "Man-in-the-Middle" trap.

Recommendation: If your device is no longer receiving security updates, it is strongly recommended that you stop using it for banking, medical records, and other sensitive transactions.
