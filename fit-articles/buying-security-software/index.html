Buying Security Software

Antivirus (or anti-malware) products have largely become unnecessary because modern operating systems now include powerful, built-in defenses right out of the box - such as Microsoft Defender on Windows, Apple's native XProtect and built-in security architecture on macOS and iOS, and Google Play Protect on Android.

Instead of filling a vital gap, many third-party security programs have devolved into "Security Suites" that duplicate native platform features while introducing unnecessary crap. (Yes, its that bad and you know it!) We call it Bloatware.

The Worst and Best for:    macOS    iOS    Win 11    Android

When highly marketed security products prioritize profits over protection, their tactics can quickly turn invasive. You end up with urgent, panic-inducing pop-ups -- falsely warning that your system is "critically vulnerable" or riddled with hundreds of "severe issues" -- just to pressure you into purchasing a new subscription upgrade or redundant software you don't need like driver updaters or system optimizers that ALL operating systems already provide. McAfee and Norton lead the list, routinely deploying aggressive scareware strategies, disguising harmless tracking cookies, minor cache files or even temporary files as infections, while sneaking unwanted browser extensions, companion utilities, and recurring auto-renewal traps onto your device without clear consent.

By contrast, streamlined tools like those that focus purely on core threat detection avoid these predatory marketing loops entirely. They respect your workflow by running quietly in the background without fake alerts, constant upsell banners, or questionable software you probably don't need. (See the links above.)

No single solution is 100% foolproof and unfortunately human error is usually the culprit, so combine a good anti-malware app with these security practices:

  • Keep your operating system and all software updated.
  • Use a strong, unique password for every online account, password length is more important than complexity, however always use at least one capital letter, one small, one number and one alpha-numeric character ( # $ * ! )
  • Be wary of suspicious emails and links.
  • Back up your important data regularly.
  • Use a reputable ad-blocker.
  • Consider a free VPN if you are using public Wi-Fi in airports, etc.
